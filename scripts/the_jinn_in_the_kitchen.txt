[cast: JEFF=hero boy, PAATI=grandma, AMMA=mum, THE GUEST=villain shadow]

# Chapter One: The Rule

My grandmother had exactly two rules for our house. Never whistle after dark. And never answer if someone calls your name twice from the kitchen at night.

I was fourteen, so naturally I thought both rules were for babies.

PAATI: Jinn love a whistle. To them, it's a doorbell.

JEFF: Paati, it's a song. Jinn don't have taste.

That night I whistled the whole way to my bedroom. Loudly. Badly. On purpose.

At three twelve in the morning, someone in the kitchen whistled it back. Perfectly.

# Chapter Two: The Guest

The next morning, the kitchen smelled of jasmine and burnt toast.

Someone had made breakfast. Six plates. We are a family of three.

AMMA: Jeff, did you cook?

JEFF: Amma, I can't even make tea.

The security camera showed the kitchen at three twelve. The light switched on by itself. A tall black shape stood at the counter, buttering toast with great care.

It turned to the camera, and two orange eyes looked straight at me. Then it waved.

Paati watched the footage twice and calmly put on her reading glasses.

PAATI: You invited a guest. Now you must host him.

JEFF: Host him? Can't we just block him?

# Chapter Three: Name Twice

For a week, things got stranger. My shoes were polished every morning. My homework was finished, in better handwriting than mine. Someone had folded my socks into tiny swans.

Honestly, it was the best week of my life.

Then, on Friday night, a voice called from the kitchen.

THE GUEST: Jeff.

I sat up in bed. The voice sounded exactly like my mother.

THE GUEST: Jeff. Come and see.

That was twice. Paati's second rule. I pulled the blanket over my head and pretended to be a pillow.

My phone buzzed. A message from Amma. I'm staying at Aunty's house tonight. Why?

# Chapter Four: The Deal

At dawn, Paati marched into the kitchen, sat down, and poured two cups of tea. One for her. One for the empty chair.

PAATI: You've had your fun. The boy whistles terribly. Go home.

The lights flickered. The shadow appeared in the chair, holding the cup with very good manners.

THE GUEST: He whistled for me seven nights. I thought we were friends.

PAATI: Everyone thinks that about Jeff, at first.

JEFF: Paati!

The guest sighed, finished his tea, and folded himself into the dark under the fridge. The jasmine smell went with him.

Paati made me promise. No more whistling. Ever.

I kept that promise for a whole month.

Then last night, I couldn't sleep, so I hummed. Just hummed. Very quietly.

From under the fridge, somebody hummed back. In harmony.
